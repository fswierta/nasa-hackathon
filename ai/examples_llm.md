## 1. Does flame spread faster in microgravity than in 1 g?

*gemini · gemini-3.5-flash-lite · from cache* — all [n] markers resolve

In microgravity, materials with adequate ventilation may burn more readily compared to normal gravity (1 g) under identical conditions [1]. Specifically, in concurrent-flow comparisons over thin fuel, the microgravity flame reaches a nearly steady-state condition with a convex base, whereas the normal-gravity flame is much longer, sootier, has a concave base, and continues to accelerate over the available fuel length [2]. Additionally, certain fuels that cannot sustain downward (opposed-flow) burning in normal gravity—extinguishing shortly after ignition—will successfully support opposed-flow burning in microgravity [2].

## 4. How does smoke differ in microgravity (SAME)?

*gemini · gemini-3.5-flash-lite · from cache* — all [n] markers resolve

In microgravity, the absence of strong buoyancy-induced flows leads to reduced velocities and mixing, higher vapor concentrations, and increased smoke residence times near the source compared to normal gravity (1-g) [1][2]. These conditions enhance nucleation, condensation, and coagulation, resulting in larger smoke particles and larger droplet sizes for liquid aerosol smokes, along with a corresponding decrease in particle number density for the same mass of smoke [1]. Additionally, low gravity increases the transit time from the reaction zone to the detector [2].

## 5. What is known about fires at Moon-base atmospheres (30–34 % O2, ~56–70 kPa)?

*gemini · gemini-3.5-flash-lite · from cache* — all [n] markers resolve

In microgravity tests simulating NASA proposed Moon-base/spacecraft atmospheres containing 34% oxygen at 0.56 atm (8.2 psia), common plastics such as PMMA (emulated with ethylene/N2 fuel mixtures having heats of combustion around 22 to 24 kJ/g and heats of gasification around 2.42 to 3 kJ/g) can steadily and continuously burn after reaching steady state in about 80 to 100 seconds [1][2]. Furthermore, tests at 34% oxygen concentrations showed that all tested flames reached steady burning [1].
