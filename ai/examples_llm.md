## 1. Does flame spread faster in microgravity than in 1 g?

*gemini · gemini-3.5-flash-lite* — all [n] markers resolve

In microgravity, certain configurations or conditions allow materials to burn more readily or spread faster compared to normal gravity. Specifically, for thin fuel samples in microgravity with an air flow speed around 10 cm/s, a concurrent-flow flame spreads twice as fast as an opposed-flow flame [1][2]. Additionally, normal-gravity concurrent-flow flames are much longer and still accelerating over the available fuel length, whereas microgravity concurrent-flow flames nearly reach steady-state conditions [2]. Furthermore, certain fuels can support opposed-flow burning in microgravity but will quickly extinguish and cannot sustain downward (opposed-flow) burning in normal gravity [2].

## 3. What O2 level stopped droplet burning in FLEX?

*gemini · gemini-3.5-flash-lite* — all [n] markers resolve

The Flame Extinguishment Experiment (FLEX) determines the Limiting Oxygen Index (LOI), which is defined as the minimum ambient oxygen concentration (or mole fraction) below which a steady flame cannot exist and droplets do not ignite [1][2][4]. Research has shown that this flammability limit is significantly lower in reduced gravity compared to normal gravity and previous predictions [3][4].

## 4. How does smoke differ in microgravity (SAME)?

*gemini · gemini-3.5-flash-lite* — all [n] markers resolve

In microgravity, the absence of buoyancy-induced flows leads to higher vapor concentrations and longer smoke residence times near the pyrolyzing or smoldering source [1][2]. These conditions enhance nucleation, condensation, and coagulation, resulting in larger smoke particles and larger liquid aerosol droplet sizes compared to normal gravity (1-g), with a corresponding decrease in particle number density for the same mass of smoke [1]. These differences in size distribution and number density can affect the response of spacecraft smoke detectors [1].
